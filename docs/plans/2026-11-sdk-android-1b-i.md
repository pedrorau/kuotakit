# Plan: the SDK for Android, subphase 1b-i (restore and recovery of purchases)

- **Origin:** context (subphase 1b was split in two by the maintainer on 2026-11-12: 1b-i recovers purchases, 1b-ii does plan changes, the offline cache, the end-to-end test and closes the public API). Reviewed against the official Play Billing documentation before writing (sources below).
- **Approved by the maintainer on:** 2026-11-12
- **Branch / PR:** `feat/sdk-android-1b-i` / not started

## Goal
No purchase the user made in the app is lost any more. Today a purchase whose receipt could not be sent stays unconfirmed (Google refunds it after three days), buying it again fails with "already owns", and a purchase that completes while no `purchase` call is waiting (a pending payment approved later, the app killed during the purchase) is never sent. After this task the SDK finds those purchases in Google Play and sends them to the backend, so the backend can confirm them. The SDK still never decides access (ADR 0003).

## What the official documentation says (read on 2026-11-12) and what it changes
Sources: [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate) (sections "Detect and process purchases", "Grant entitlement", "Attach user identifiers"), [`BillingResponseCode`](https://developer.android.com/reference/com/android/billingclient/api/BillingClient.BillingResponseCode), [`Purchase`](https://developer.android.com/reference/com/android/billingclient/api/Purchase), [`AccountIdentifiers`](https://developer.android.com/reference/com/android/billingclient/api/AccountIdentifiers), [deprecation FAQ](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/deprecation-faq).
- **Purchases are detected in two ways, and the app must do both.** `onPurchasesUpdated` (while a connection is open: also for a purchase made outside the app or a pending one that completes) and `queryPurchasesAsync` when the connection is made, "critical" for network failures during the purchase, several devices, purchases made outside the app and pending ones completed while the app was closed. So the SDK needs a **sync** (query the owned purchases and send them) and must also act on updates that arrive when no `purchase` call is waiting.
- **`ITEM_ALREADY_OWNED` is meant to be handled by fetching purchases:** Google says to bring purchases up to date with the fetching guidance; if the error appears anyway, Play's cache was stale, the error refreshes it, and the purchases can then be processed. This confirms the agreement of 2026-11-12: on that code the SDK syncs.
- **Only `PURCHASED` is granted; `PENDING` is not.** A pending purchase is processed only when it becomes `PURCHASED` (an update arrives then). The 1a code already does this for the purchase flow; the sync must do it too.
- **`Purchase.getAccountIdentifiers()` returns the obfuscated account id that the app attached** and "purchases made outside your app won't have these identifiers set". The backend refuses a purchase without our account id (ADR 0017), so the sync **sends only the purchases whose account id is the hash of the current app user id**; sending the others would only produce `402` and `409` and spend the request budget. A purchase made outside the app, or under another app user id (another device, a reinstall), cannot be recovered in v0: it is the known limit of ADR 0017, and identity (logIn) is out of v0 (ADR 0005). The SDK says so in the error instead of staying silent.
- **Version policy of the Play Billing Library (for the question of `minSdk`):** each major version is accepted for new apps and updates for about two years: 7 until 2026-08-31 (extension until 2026-11-01, both already past), 8 until 2027-08-31, 9 until 2028-08-31. The policy says nothing about `minSdk`. Measured on the AARs: 9.1.0 and 8.3.0 both have `minSdkVersion` 23 (7.1.1 has 21 but is no longer accepted). So **23 is the lowest `minSdk` any acceptable version allows; the SDK keeps 23** and nothing is to be gained from a lower one.
- **What I could not confirm in the documentation:** whether `queryPurchasesAsync` needs the network or answers from the cache of the Play Store app on the device. The design does not depend on it: a failed query is an error, never "no purchases".

## Scope
- **`restore()` in the core:** `suspend fun restore(): RestoreOutcome(customerInfo, submitted, refused)`. It asks the billing adapter for the purchases the user owns, keeps the ones that are `PURCHASED`, of this app and carrying this app user's account id, sends each receipt to the backend (one at a time) and then reads the customer info from the backend, which is the answer. A purchase the backend refuses (`402`, `409`) counts as `refused` and does not stop the others; a transport failure (`Network`, `StoreUnavailable`, `RateLimited`, `Unauthorized`) stops the restore with its exception, and nothing is lost: the purchases are still in Google Play for the next sync.
- **`BillingAdapter` gains** `suspend ownedPurchases(): List<OwnedPurchase>` and `purchaseUpdates: Flow<OwnedPurchase>` (a purchase that arrives when no `purchase` call is waiting), with `OwnedPurchase(receipt, state, accountId)` and `OwnedState` (`Purchased`, `Pending`). `BillingResult` gains `AlreadyOwned`.
- **`purchase()` on `AlreadyOwned`:** the SDK runs a restore and returns `PurchaseOutcome.AlreadyOwned(customerInfo)` when it sent at least one purchase; if none of the owned purchases carries this app user's account id it throws `InvalidRequest` saying that the purchase was made under another app user id.
- **Background sync:** the core listens to `purchaseUpdates` and syncs when one arrives, and syncs once after `configure`. One sync at a time (a lock), failures are kept for the next trigger and never shown as success. There are no timers or retries of its own: the triggers are `configure`, an update, `ITEM_ALREADY_OWNED` and an explicit `restore()` that the app calls when it comes to the foreground (documented), as Google recommends for the moment of connecting.
- **A layer of our own over `BillingClient` (agreed 2026-11-12):** `PlayClient` (connect, look up a product as `SUBS` then `INAPP`, launch the flow, query owned purchases, a flow of updates) with our own types, and a thin class that only translates to and from Google's types. The logic that was in `BillingClientPlayClient` (order of lookups, reading the result of a failed query, the product match, the explicit `PURCHASED` check, the waiter) moves into a class that is unit tested with a fake `PlayClient`. Only the translation and the call to Google stay for a device test.
- **The connection:** the client keeps one connection and connects when needed, as today; the sync connects too.
- **Tests, ADR 0019 amended (or ADR 0020 if the change is large), `docs/sdk-android.md`, AGENTS.md, the ABI dump, a Konsist canary if a rule changes.**

## Out of scope (1b-ii or later)
- Plan changes (upgrades and downgrades) that carry the account id, the offline cache of the customer info, the end-to-end test against the reference backend in Docker, and closing the public API: subphase 1b-ii.
- Recovering purchases made outside the app or under another app user id (needs identity, out of v0).
- Timers or exponential retries inside the SDK; WorkManager; observing the app lifecycle (the app calls `restore()` when it comes to the foreground).
- The example app and a real device, the iOS adapter, bridges, publishing.

## State model
- **What a sync does with an owned purchase** is explicit, decided in one place of the core: a sealed type with `Send` (purchased, this app, this account id), `Waiting` (pending), `NotOurs` (no account id, or another one, or another package) and `Ignore` (any other state, which is never sent). No boolean flags that have to agree.
- **The sync itself** is idle or running, held by one lock; nothing else holds state (the purchases live in Google Play, the truth stays in the backend). No timers, so there is no retry state to model.
- **`PurchaseOutcome` and `BillingResult`** gain `AlreadyOwned`; both stay sealed types defined in one place.

## Decisions taken
- **Google Play is the queue of purchases to send:** no purchase is stored by the SDK. A purchase that could not be sent is found again in the next sync. Alternative left out: an own persistent queue (more state, more places to disagree, and Google already keeps the purchase until it is acknowledged or refunded).
- **Only purchases with this app user's account id are sent** (agent, from the documentation and ADR 0017). Alternative left out: send everything and let the backend refuse (spends the request budget and gives noisy errors).
- **`restore()` returns the customer info of the backend, read after the receipts** (agent): a restore with nothing to send still answers what the backend knows.
- **No timers in the SDK** (agent): the app calls `restore()` when it comes to the foreground, as Google recommends syncing when connecting. Alternative left out: observing the process lifecycle or WorkManager (platform dependencies and more state).
- **`minSdk` stays 23** (measured above).

Six negative checks failed the tests as they should and were reverted: sending purchases of another account, sending a pending purchase, stopping the restore at the first refusal, two syncs at once, a failed query read as "nothing owned", and forwarding updates while a purchase call waits.
- Differences from the plan: a `400` from the backend also counts as `refused` (it is about that purchase, and stopping would leave the others unsent); the "another package" case is not classified, because Play only returns purchases of this app; listening to updates starts together with the first sync (not after it) so that an update during the first sync is not missed; `ConcurrentHashMap.newKeySet` was replaced because Android lint found it needs API 24 and the SDK supports 23.
- 2026-11-12: the CI run on `main` after the merge of #16 failed in the `ios` job (and so in `ci`): `KuotaKitTest.anOlderAnswerNeverOverwritesANewerOne` on the iOS simulator. It passed in the pull request and in six local runs: the test decided which request was the slow one by the order in which they reached the mock engine, which depends on which engine thread gets there first (the runner has 3 processors). A race of the test, not of the SDK (the stamps are issued in the order of the calls). The test now starts the second request only after the first has reached the engine; its negative check (a stale answer overwriting a newer one) still fails it. This is the only edit of a merged test not caused by an intentional behaviour change; no assertion changed.
- 2026-11-12: independent review (a read-only subagent): no path where the SDK reports access without the backend, and 15 findings; fixed: a waiting purchase call took any update (now only one that answers it; the others are forwarded), repeated sends spent the request budget (a purchase the backend already answered is not sent again in the same run), `AlreadyOwned` could be returned for another product or with a false message (now by product, and `PurchaseInvalid` if the backend refused), an unexpected exception in the background could end the app (everything but cancellation is caught), `configure` twice left the old sync running (stopped), concurrent connection attempts (a lock), updates beyond the buffer (the newest is kept), a subscription with no offer token, and `restore()` had no count of unattributable purchases (`RestoreOutcome.unattributed`). Tests for each, five more negative checks. Recorded as limits in the ADR: a `purchase` call has no time limit, `copy`/`componentN` and `OwnedState.OTHER` to be reconsidered in 1b-ii. Not changed: `InvalidRequest` counted as refused is limited to what `submitReceipt` throws (the review's worry about an SDK `InvalidRequest` does not apply, those are thrown before the receipt step). One test of 1a changed because the behaviour changed on purpose: `onlyAPurchasedPurchaseOfTheProductThatWasAskedForIsAReceipt` no longer expects an error for an update of another product, which is now ignored by the waiting call (and has its own test).
